# Business Credit Cards and the 5/24 Rule: Which Issuers Don't Report

Most business credit cards do **not** show up on your personal credit report as new accounts, which means they don't add to your 5/24 count. The major issuers that follow this pattern include American Express, Chase, Citi, Bank of America, and Barclays. Capital One and Discover are the notable exceptions — their business cards typically do report to personal bureaus.

---

## Which business card issuers actually stay off your personal report?

The short answer: Amex, Chase, Citi, Bank of America, and Barclays business cards generally do not add to your 5/24 count because they don't report new accounts to personal credit bureaus. This has been [consistently documented through community data points on r/churning](https://www.reddit.com/r/churning/wiki/index) and corroborated by years of hobbyist tracking.

Here's a practical breakdown of the major issuers:

**American Express** business cards do not report to personal bureaus as new accounts. The Amex Business Platinum, Business Gold, and Blue Business Cash are all fair game from a 5/24 standpoint. Amex does perform a hard pull on your personal credit when you apply, so you'll see an inquiry, but the account itself won't appear as a new tradeline on your personal report.

**Chase** business cards work the same way. The Ink Business Preferred, Ink Business Cash, and Ink Business Unlimited won't add to your 5/24 count after approval. This is actually one of the more useful quirks in the hobby: you can hold multiple Ink cards without burning through your precious 5/24 slots.

**Citi, Bank of America, and Barclays** follow the same general pattern. The Citi AAdvantage Business card, the Bank of America Business Advantage cards, and Barclays business products typically don't show up as new personal accounts.

---

## What about Capital One and Discover?

These two are the exceptions. Capital One business cards [report to all three personal credit bureaus](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/credit-cards/does-business-credit-card-affect-personal-credit), which means the Venture X Business or Spark cards will add to your 5/24 count just like a personal card would. Discover business cards behave similarly.

If you're managing a tight 5/24 strategy, many people find it makes sense to treat Capital One and Discover business applications the same way they'd treat a personal card application: as a slot being used.

---

## Does applying for a business card still generate a hard inquiry?

Yes, almost universally. Even issuers whose business cards don't report as new accounts will still pull your personal credit when you apply. That hard inquiry shows up on your report and can temporarily affect your score, but it does not count toward 5/24 because Chase looks at *new accounts*, not inquiries.

The distinction matters. You could apply for five Amex business cards in a year, rack up five hard pulls, and still be sitting at 0/24 as far as Chase is concerned (assuming no personal cards were opened). Your score might take a few small hits from the inquiries, but your application eligibility for Chase products stays intact.

---

## Does this mean business cards are completely invisible to Chase?

Not entirely. Chase can still see your business card accounts when they do a full credit review, even if those accounts don't count toward 5/24. During a reconsideration call or a credit line review, a Chase analyst has access to your full credit file, which may include business tradelines depending on how the issuer reports.

More practically: Chase's own business cards (the Ink line) don't count toward 5/24, but Chase does consider your overall relationship and existing card portfolio when making approval decisions. Having six Ink cards already won't disqualify you automatically, but [data points suggest Chase has become more selective about stacking Ink cards](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/chase-ink-business-cards-approval-odds-and-data-points/) in recent years, particularly for applicants who appear to be cycling through bonuses.

---

## How do I know if a specific card will report to my personal credit?

The most reliable method is checking [Doctor of Credit's issuer-specific guides](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/which-business-credit-cards-report-to-personal-credit-bureaus/) and the data point threads on r/churning. Doctor of Credit maintains a regularly updated breakdown of which business cards report to personal bureaus, organized by issuer.

A common approach is to pull your personal credit reports from [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com) a few months after opening a business card to confirm whether it appeared. If the account shows up as a new tradeline on your Experian, Equifax, or TransUnion report, it counted toward 5/24.

---

## What's the actual strategy here?

For anyone working around 5/24, business cards from non-reporting issuers are one of the most effective tools available. A common sequence many people use looks something like this:

1. Get approved for Chase personal and Ink business cards while under 5/24
2. Once at or near 4/24, shift focus to Amex, Citi, and Bank of America business cards
3. Collect sign-up bonuses on those products without moving the 5/24 counter
4. Let personal card accounts age off the 24-month window naturally
5. Re-enter the Chase ecosystem when slots open back up

This isn't a loophole that's going away. It's simply how business credit reporting works, and issuers like Amex have structured their products this way deliberately. The business card market operates on different credit reporting norms than consumer cards, and that structure benefits anyone who understands it.

The key constraint most people run into isn't the reporting rules, it's the business card application requirements themselves. You need at least a plausible business entity, a consistent address, and an EIN or SSN to apply. Sole proprietors with side income qualify in most cases, but that's a topic for a separate post.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do business credit cards count toward Chase 5/24?
Most business credit cards do **not** count toward your Chase 5/24 slot total. Issuers like American Express, Bank of America, Chase, Citi, and Wells Fargo typically don't report business card accounts to personal credit bureaus, meaning they won't appear on your personal credit report or increment your 5/24 count. However, you still need to be **under** 5/24 to get approved for Chase business cards in the first place.

### Which business credit cards don't show up on my personal credit report?
Business cards from American Express, Chase, Citi, Bank of America, and Barclays generally don't report to personal credit bureaus and won't affect your 5/24 count. Capital One and Discover are notable exceptions — they **do** report business card activity to personal bureaus, which means those accounts add to your 5/24 total. Always verify current reporting practices before applying, as issuer policies can change.

### Can I still apply for Chase business cards if I'm over 5/24?
No — being over 5/24 will disqualify you from Chase business card approvals, even though those cards won't add to your count afterward. Chase applies the 5/24 rule at the point of application for both personal and business products. The strategic advantage of business cards is that once approved, they let you earn sign-up bonuses without consuming one of your precious 5/24 slots.

### What is the best order to apply for credit cards to stay under 5/24?
The most effective strategy is to prioritize Chase personal and business cards first, then move to other issuers. Since Chase is the strictest about 5/24, apply for Chase products while your count is low. Next, add non-reporting business cards from Amex, Citi, or Bank of America to accumulate bonuses without raising your count. Finish with cards from issuers like Capital One that report to personal bureaus.

### Does applying for a business credit card trigger a hard inquiry on my personal credit?
Yes — most business card applications still result in a hard inquiry on your personal credit report, even if the account itself isn't reported. This means the inquiry is visible but the open account line typically won't count toward 5/24 for non-reporting issuers. Hard inquiries have a minor, temporary impact on your score and disappear from your report after two years, making them far less consequential than a new account slot.
