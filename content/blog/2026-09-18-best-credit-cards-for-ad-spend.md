# Best Credit Cards For Ad Spend: Common Mistakes To Avoid

Ad spend can push you past 5/24 or unlock huge bonuses fast. But most people pick the wrong card, code the spend wrong, or trip a fraud flag. Use a business card built for large recurring charges, track your bonus categories, and call your issuer before you scale up spend.

## Are You Using A Personal Card Instead Of A Business Card?

This is the biggest mistake. Many people run ad spend through a personal cash back card because it is the one they already have open. That leaves real points on the table.

Ad spend is business spend. Even if you are a solo freelancer or run a small side hustle, you likely qualify for a business card. Business cards often have higher rewards on advertising categories and higher credit limits. That second point matters more than people think. A personal card with a $5,000 limit gets maxed out fast if you are running $8,000 a month in Facebook ads. A business card built for higher spend avoids that ceiling problem entirely.

Cards like the Chase Ink Business Cash and Ink Business Preferred both offer bonus categories tied to advertising purchases through social media sites and search engines. The Ink Business Cash offers 5% back on the first $25,000 combined each account year in categories that include internet, cable, and phone services, plus some versions of ad spend depending on how the charge codes. The American Express Business Gold Card automatically shifts its 4x bonus categories to match your top spending categories each month, which often includes advertising if that is where your dollars go.

If you do not have a business entity, that is fine. Sole proprietors can apply for business cards using their own name and Social Security number. You do not need an LLC or a registered business name.

**How to avoid it:** Open a business card before you scale up ad spend, not after. Getting the card first means you build spend history on the right product from day one, instead of retraining your habits later.

## Are You Assuming All Ad Platforms Code The Same Way?

They do not. Facebook, Google, TikTok, and Amazon ads can all code differently depending on how the platform processes payments, and that changes which bonus category you actually earn.

This trips people up constantly. A card that offers bonus points on "advertising purchases made with select media companies" sounds broad, but the fine print usually names specific platforms. The Ink Business Preferred, for example, has historically listed social media sites and search engines as bonus categories, but not every ad platform falls under those labels. Amazon ads, for instance, may code as a general retail purchase rather than advertising, depending on the card issuer's system.

This means the same $3,000 Facebook ad bill might earn 5x on one card and 1x on another, purely based on how the merchant category code (MCC) gets assigned. MCCs are set by the payment processor, not the card issuer, so even Chase or Amex cannot always guarantee how a specific charge will be coded.

**How to avoid it:** Check your statement after your first ad charge posts. Look at what category it earned, not just what you expected. If a card is not earning the bonus rate you planned on, move future ad spend to a different card that same billing cycle. Many people in the hobby keep a running note of which platform codes as what, updated whenever they open a new card or notice a change.

## Are You Ignoring Spending Limits On Bonus Categories?

Bonus categories almost always have a spending cap. Once you hit it, your rewards rate drops, sometimes back down to 1x. Ad spend can burn through that cap in a single month if you are running a real campaign budget.

The Ink Business Cash caps its 5% categories at $25,000 combined per account year. Split across multiple bonus categories, that is not much room if you are spending $10,000 a month on ads alone. Once you cross the cap, every extra dollar earns the base rate, which is usually just 1%.

People get surprised by this because they set up the card, see a great return in month one, and assume it holds steady. Then month three hits, the cap is gone, and the return quietly drops without any alert from the issuer.

**How to avoid it:** Track your bonus category spend against the cap every month. A simple spreadsheet works fine. Once you are within a few thousand dollars of the cap, many people shift new ad spend to a second card, either another bonus category card or a flat-rate card like the Ink Business Unlimited, which earns 1.5% on everything with no cap at all.

## Are You Triggering Fraud Holds With Sudden Spend Spikes?

Yes, this happens often, and it is one of the more frustrating mistakes. A new card with a $10,000 limit that suddenly gets hit with $8,000 in Facebook ad charges in one week can trigger an automatic fraud hold, even if you have perfect credit and a long history with the issuer.

Card issuers use algorithms to flag unusual activity. A brand new account with no spending history, followed by a huge charge to an online ad platform, looks a lot like stolen card fraud from the issuer's point of view. Digital ad platforms are common targets for fraudsters because the "goods" are intangible and hard to claw back once spent.

This mistake happens most right after opening a new card. People want to hit a big minimum spend requirement fast, so they load up the ad account immediately. The issuer sees a $0 balance jump to $9,000 overnight and locks the card pending verification.

**How to avoid it:** Ramp up gradually during the first billing cycle. Many people start with a smaller ad charge, let it post, then increase spend over the following days. If you know you are about to run a large campaign, some people call the issuer ahead of time to give a heads up. It does not guarantee a smooth ride, but it lowers the odds of an interruption mid-campaign.

## Are You Forgetting That Ad Platforms Charge In Batches, Not Real Time?

Facebook and Google do not always charge your card the moment you set your budget. Depending on your billing threshold and account history, they may bill daily, weekly, or in a lump sum that includes several days of spend at once.

This matters for minimum spend timing. If you are trying to hit a $6,000 minimum spend requirement by a certain date, but your ad account bills in a lump sum every 30 days, you might miss the deadline even though you technically "spent" the money on ads that ran earlier.

It also matters for cash flow. A sudden batch charge for $4,000 can catch you off guard if you were expecting smaller, spread-out charges.

**How to avoid it:** Check your ad platform's billing settings before you rely on it to hit a minimum spend deadline. Facebook Ads Manager and Google Ads both show your billing threshold and payment history in their billing settings page. If your deadline is tight, many people pad in a buffer of a week or two, just in case a charge posts later than expected.

## Are You Putting All Your Eggs In One Bonus Category Card?

Relying on a single card for all ad spend limits your rewards and increases risk. If that card gets flagged, hits its cap, or the bonus category shifts, your whole strategy stalls out.

Some advertisers run large monthly budgets, sometimes tens of thousands of dollars across multiple platforms. Putting all of that on one card means one problem, whether it is a fraud hold, a maxed out limit, or a lapsed bonus cap, stops your rewards earning entirely.

A more resilient setup spreads spend across two or three business cards. One handles the bonus category up to its cap. A second, flat-rate card picks up the overflow. A third can serve as backup if either of the first two gets flagged or frozen.

**How to avoid it:** Treat ad spend like any other large recurring expense; do not centralize it on one card. Many people in the hobby keep a primary bonus card, a flat 1.5% or 2% back card for overflow, and a backup card sitting unused but active, ready to step in if needed.

## Are You Forgetting To Track How Ad Spend Affects Your 5/24 Status?

Every new business or personal card you open to optimize ad spend still counts toward your Chase 5/24 count, if it is a card that reports to your personal credit file. Some people get so focused on maximizing categories that they lose track of how many cards they have opened in the last 24 months.

Chase counts most personal and business cards from any bank toward your 5/24 total, with a few exceptions like certain small business cards from Chase itself, which sometimes do not report to personal credit bureaus. But cards from other issuers, like Amex or Capital One, generally do count against you, even if they are business cards.

If you are chasing ad spend rewards across three or four new cards in a short window, you might unknowingly push yourself over the 5/24 line, closing the door on future Chase products you actually wanted more.

**How to avoid it:** Before opening any new card for ad spend purposes, check your current 5/24 count. Many people prioritize Chase cards first, before opening cards from other issuers, so they do not accidentally lock themselves out of Chase's own lineup later.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best credit card for ad spend on Facebook and Google?
The Chase Ink Business Preferred is generally the best card for ad spend because it earns 3x points on the first $150,000 combined spent on categories including advertising purchases made with social media sites and search engines each account anniversary year. Points transfer to Chase Ultimate Rewards partners like United and Hyatt at full value. If you're outside Chase's 5/24 window or want flat-rate simplicity, the Capital One Spark Cash Plus or American Express Business Gold are strong alternatives with high earning rates on ad spend.

### How does the Chase 5/24 rule affect getting an ad spend credit card?
Chase 5/24 means you'll be denied for Chase cards, including Ink Business Preferred, if you've opened 5 or more personal or business credit cards (from any bank) in the past 24 months. This makes sequencing critical for marketers and agencies who want Chase's ad spend bonus categories. Apply for Chase business cards early in your credit card strategy, before pursuing cards from Amex, Capital One, or other issuers that don't have similar restrictions.

### Should I apply for Chase Ink Business Preferred before other cards?
Yes, apply for Chase Ink Business Preferred first if you're under 5/24 and plan to build a card portfolio. Since Chase strictly enforces the 5/24 rule and other issuers like Amex and Capital One are more lenious about it, prioritizing Chase applications preserves your eligibility. This is especially important for agencies or businesses with heavy ad spend, since missing the Ink Preferred's 3x bonus category and welcome offer due to being over 5/24 is a costly mistake.

### Can I get a large sign-up bonus by putting ad spend on a new credit card?
Yes, ad spend is one of the fastest ways to hit minimum spending requirements for large sign-up bonuses, since agencies and businesses often spend thousands monthly on advertising. Cards like Ink Business Preferred (100,000 points after $8,000 spend) or Amex Business Gold can be met in a single billing cycle with substantial ad budgets. Just confirm the platform (Meta, Google, TikTok) codes as a bonus-eligible category, and pay balances in full to avoid interest costs outweighing point value.

### Is it better to use points or cash back for business ad spend rewards?
It depends on how you'll redeem them: transferable points (Chase Ultimate Rewards, Amex Membership Rewards) typically offer higher value, often 1.5-2+ cents per point, when redeemed for travel through transfer partners. Cash back cards like Capital One Spark Cash Plus offer simplicity with a flat 2% return, which is better if you don't want to manage transfer partners or book award travel. High-volume ad spenders usually get more value from points if they redeem strategically.
